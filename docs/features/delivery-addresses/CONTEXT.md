# Domain Context — delivery-addresses

## Glossary

- Address Drift — The Drift Signal reported against a Ready for Ordering Purchase Draft when a Customer Order its line links to is now going to a different Delivery Address than the one frozen for that link when the draft entered Ready for Ordering. Like every other Drift Signal it compares the frozen draft against current demand, reports the divergence, never rewrites the frozen draft, and stops reporting once the addresses agree again. Because the comparison is against the demand's own frozen address rather than the line's, it is as meaningful on a Via Warehouse line as on a Direct to Customer one; it is reported on the Purchase Draft list itself for a Direct to Customer line, where it means goods are travelling to the wrong place, and when the draft is opened for a Via Warehouse line, where everything still lands at one dock. NOT an error, and NOT an instruction; what to do about it is the member's decision, and it never blocks closing the draft.
- Customer — A record of one named end customer within one Warehouse, carrying the Delivery Addresses that customer's goods may be sent to and the demand that customer is waiting for. Its name is unique within its Warehouse, it is active when created, and it may be made Inactive and active again. NOT the customer name typed onto a Customer Order before this release, which remains on every Customer Order recorded that way and is never rewritten into a Customer by the system. NOT a party with pricing, payment terms, or contact people, none of which this release models. NOT a record shared across the Warehouses of one Workspace; each Warehouse keeps its own.
- Delivery Address — A place goods can be sent to, belonging either to a Customer or to the Warehouse itself, recorded as text the member types together with the access notes a driver needs. It is active when created and may be made Inactive and active again. NOT a Location, which a later release introduces as a modelled place inside a Warehouse where Stock is held; a Delivery Address answers "where do these goods travel to", a Location answers "where did I put them". NOT the web "destination" naming used for routed screens, which is presentation vocabulary and carries no domain meaning. NOT a validated, geocoded, or carrier-resolvable address; this release neither verifies nor interprets what the member typed.
- Delivery Mode — Which of the two ways the goods of one Purchase Draft Line travel: Via Warehouse or Direct to Customer. It is chosen per line rather than per draft, so one Purchase Draft may hold lines of both modes, and it is frozen with the rest of the line at Ready for Ordering. NOT a Packaging Type, which says how the goods are physically presented rather than where they go. NOT a shipping method or carrier selection, which this release does not model.
- Direct Delivery — The act by which a Warehouse Member records that the goods of a Direct to Customer Purchase Draft Line reached the customer, stating the delivered quantity and its Allocation across the Customer Orders the line is linked to. It reduces Outstanding Quantity exactly as an Arrival Confirmation does and changes On-hand Quantity no more than one does. NOT an Arrival Confirmation, which records goods that physically reached the Transit Zone; the two are refused against each other's Delivery Mode. NOT a dispatch, a carrier handover, or a proof of delivery, none of which this release provides — the member records what the customer told them and the system does not inspect it.
- Direct to Customer — The Delivery Mode in which the supplier sends a Purchase Draft Line's goods straight to a Customer's Delivery Address. The goods never enter the Warehouse, are never counted in its On-hand Quantity, and are never held in its Transit Zone. A line in this mode ships to a Customer's Delivery Address and never to the Warehouse's own, and it links only to Customer Orders going to that same Delivery Address. NOT Via Warehouse, which is the existing path and remains the default.
- Inactive — The state of a Customer, or of one of a Customer's Delivery Addresses, that a member has deactivated because it should no longer be used. An Inactive Customer is not offered when demand is recorded, and an Inactive Delivery Address is not offered when a Delivery Address is chosen, while every Customer Order and Purchase Draft Line already naming either stays readable and keeps counting exactly as before, and an Inactive Customer's name stays taken. Deactivating a Customer leaves its Delivery Addresses in whatever state they were already in, and a Customer may be deactivated whether or not it is still waiting for goods. NOT a state the Warehouse Delivery Address can be put into, which is corrected in place instead. NOT deleted, which this release does not provide. NOT a merge of two Customers naming one company, which is out of scope.
- Main Delivery Address — The one Delivery Address of a Customer that a Customer Order takes when the member states none. Exactly one active Delivery Address of a Customer is its Main one at any moment: a member changes which by marking another as Main, and deactivating the Main one makes one of the remaining active addresses Main automatically and tells the member which. NOT the only address a Customer may be sent goods at, since a member may state a different one on any single Customer Order.
- Redirection — The act by which a Warehouse Member changes which of its Customer's active Delivery Addresses an outstanding Customer Order is going to. It never changes which Customer the order names, is refused against an Inactive Delivery Address and against a Fulfilled or cancelled Customer Order, and reports Address Drift on every frozen Purchase Draft Line that was frozen expecting the previous address. NOT a change to a frozen Purchase Draft Line, which redirection never reaches. NOT a dispatch or a re-route of goods already travelling, which this release does not model.
- Via Warehouse — The Delivery Mode in which the supplier delivers a Purchase Draft Line's goods to the Warehouse's own Delivery Address, where they rest in the Transit Zone before going out to the customer. It is the existing behaviour of every Purchase Draft Line and remains the default. NOT Direct to Customer, in which the goods never enter the building.
- Warehouse Delivery Address — The one Delivery Address of the Warehouse itself, together with its access notes, which is what a supplier is told for a Via Warehouse line. A Warehouse has exactly one; it has the same shape as a Customer's Delivery Address so that members learn one thing rather than two, and it is corrected in place and never made Inactive. NOT a collection like a Customer's Delivery Addresses, so it has no Main one. NOT a Location, and NOT the Transit Zone, which is the physical part of the Warehouse the goods rest in rather than the address a supplier delivers to.

## Invariants

- Every Customer and every Delivery Address belongs to exactly one Warehouse, and every operation on one is authorized by the Role its actor holds in that Warehouse. Nothing crosses that boundary, and a Customer Order names only a Delivery Address of a Customer of its own Warehouse.
- A Customer's name identifies at most one Customer within a Warehouse, and the same name in two Warehouses names two unrelated Customers. Deactivating a Customer never releases its name for reuse.
- A Customer's name may be corrected at any time to a name unused in its Warehouse, and correcting it never changes which Customer Orders and Purchase Draft Lines name that Customer.
- A Customer Order names exactly one Delivery Address, which is the Customer's Main Delivery Address unless the member stated another when recording it.
- A Customer that has Unfulfilled Customer Orders always has at least one active Delivery Address, and exactly one active Delivery Address of a Customer is its Main one.
- A Purchase Draft Line carries exactly one Delivery Mode. A Direct to Customer line names a Delivery Address of a Customer and never the Warehouse Delivery Address; a Via Warehouse line goes to the Warehouse Delivery Address.
- A Direct to Customer Purchase Draft Line is linked only to Customer Orders that name the same Delivery Address it ships to, and that agreement is required whenever the link is made or the line is revised, and again when the draft enters Ready for Ordering. A Via Warehouse line keeps the existing loose linking, in which a link claims nothing and no quantity has to reconcile.
- Entering Ready for Ordering freezes each line's Delivery Mode and Delivery Address with the rest of the draft, and neither is ever changed afterwards. Divergence from where the demand now goes is reported as Address Drift instead.
- A Purchase Draft Line's ending is recorded once: an Arrival Confirmation for a Via Warehouse line and a Direct Delivery for a Direct to Customer line. Recording either against the other Delivery Mode is refused. The Purchase Draft reaches Closed once every one of its lines has had its ending recorded.
- Neither an Arrival Confirmation nor a Direct Delivery changes On-hand Quantity, which moves only through a member setting it to a counted figure while stating a reason.
- A Customer Order recorded before this release keeps the customer name it was typed with, names no Customer and no Delivery Address, and is never matched to a Customer by the system.

## Out of scope

- Locations, Stock balances, Stock Movements, and put-away; a Delivery Address answers where goods travel to, while a Location answers where they were put inside the building, and that capability depends on a movement ledger this release does not have.
- Suppliers as records, supplier addresses, and any electronic exchange with a supplier; a Delivery Address is where goods go, not who sends them.
- Dispatching Via Warehouse goods onward to the customer, carriers, tracking numbers, and shipping cost; this release records where goods were sent and that they got there, and models no dispatch operation.
- Address validation, geocoding, maps, distance, and any structured address format; a Delivery Address is text a member types and the system never interprets it.
- Proof that a Direct Delivery actually happened; as with a Pre-receipt Requirement, the member records what they know and the system does not inspect it.
- Sharing Customers or Delivery Addresses across the Warehouses of one Workspace, and merging two Customers that turn out to name one company.
