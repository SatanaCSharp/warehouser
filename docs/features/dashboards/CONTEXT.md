# Domain Context — dashboards

Every role and every domain object this feature reads is defined where it is owned and is not
repeated here: `Warehouse Member`, `Role` and `Permission` in
[`access`](../access/CONTEXT.md); `Workspace`, `Workspace Member`, `Workspace Owner`,
`Workspace Role`, `Workspace Permission`, `Active Warehouse` and `Archived Warehouse` in
[`workspaces`](../workspaces/CONTEXT.md); `Item`, `SKU`, `Customer Order`, `Outstanding Quantity`,
`Unfulfilled`, `Fulfilled`, `On-hand Quantity`, `Demand Line`, `Coverage`, `Purchase Draft`,
`Purchase Draft Line`, `Ready for Ordering`, `Discarded`, `Expected Arrival Date`,
`Arrival Confirmation` and `Allocation` in [`ordering`](../ordering/CONTEXT.md); `Rejection`,
`Rejection Reason`, `Rejection Source`, `Disposition`, `Rejected Quantity`, `Received Quantity` and
`Pre-receipt Conformance` in [`arrival-inspection`](../arrival-inspection/CONTEXT.md);
`Delivery Mode`, `Via Warehouse`, `Direct to Customer`, `Direct Delivery` and `Customer` in
[`delivery-addresses`](../delivery-addresses/CONTEXT.md).

This feature stores nothing and judges nothing. Every term below names a figure read from those
records at the moment a member looks at it.

## Glossary

- Warehouse Dashboard — The landing view of one Warehouse: four Panels read from that Warehouse's own Items, Customer Orders, Purchase Drafts and Rejections, answering what is happening at this dock. It replaces the placeholder the Warehouse's address has shown until now and is reached by entering the Warehouse rather than by a destination of its own. NOT a report a member configures, saves or schedules; the four Panels are fixed and the member chooses nothing. NOT a destination in the Warehouse's navigation beside Demand and Items, which each remain the place a member goes to act.
- Workspace Dashboard — A Workspace-level view holding four Panels that set the Workspace's Warehouses beside one another, answering which Warehouse needs attention. It is read by a Workspace Member holding the Workspace Permission that admits it, whether or not they hold a membership in any of the Warehouses it reads. NOT a consolidation of the Warehouses into one figure per Item, which `ordering` excludes and this feature does not reopen — it compares Warehouses and pools no Item. NOT the Workspace administration destination, which governs roles, members and the Warehouse lifecycle rather than what the Warehouses are doing.
- Panel — One chart on one Dashboard, together with the reads that produce it. A Panel is present when the actor holds every Permission its figures are drawn from and absent when they do not, so a Dashboard is the Panels its reader is permitted rather than a fixed grid with gaps in it. NOT a widget a member adds, removes, resizes or reorders. NOT a tile that reports a single number, each Panel being a chart with at least two dimensions.
- Arrival Timing — The Panel that sets what one Warehouse owes each week beside what it expects to reach its dock each week, over one bucket for everything Overdue and the eight weeks following. The two figures are read from different records and are never netted against each other: one is demand, the other is goods on order. NOT a forecast, nothing here predicting either side. NOT a promise that the expected goods cover the demand of the week they land in, which no record links them to.
- Purchasing Pipeline — The Panel that counts one Warehouse's open Purchase Drafts by the state they stand in and the Age Band they have stood there for. It counts drafts, never quantities, because what it reports is unfinished work rather than goods. NOT the Coverage Gap's Inbound Quantity, which counts what was ordered on those drafts rather than how many of them there are.
- Purchasing Spread — The Workspace Panel that counts every Warehouse's Purchase Drafts against every state a draft can stand in, Draft and Ready for Ordering together with Closed and Discarded, so that a Warehouse which abandons most of what it starts is distinguishable from one that cannot get goods. It counts drafts, never quantities. NOT the Purchasing Pipeline, which is the Warehouse Panel, reads only the open states, and divides them by Age Band rather than by Warehouse. NOT a judgement of a Warehouse: a Discarded draft records a decision a member made, and the Panel says where purchasing work ends up rather than whether ending it there was right.
- Order Flow — The Panel that reads one Workspace's Customer Orders by the week each was recorded, showing for each of the last twelve weeks the quantity recorded in that week and how much of that same quantity has since been assigned to arrived goods or cancelled. Every figure belongs to the week the Customer Order was recorded, never to the week the assignment or the cancellation happened, which is what lets the assigned part be a part of the recorded whole. NOT a throughput figure, which would count events in the week they occurred and could not be a part of anything. NOT a comparison of Warehouses: it pools the Workspace's Warehouses and names none of them, being the one Workspace Panel that reports the Workspace's own trend while the other three set its Warehouses beside one another.
- Recorded Quantity — The quantity one Customer Order asks for, as it stands when the Panel is read, placed in the week that Customer Order was first recorded, whether or not it has since been cancelled — a week reports what it took on, and a cancellation is a withdrawal from that whole rather than proof the week never took it on. A member who amends a Customer Order changes it, and no record keeps what it said before, so a past week's total may read differently tomorrow than it does today. NOT the quantity as it stood when the week closed, which nothing stores. NOT Outstanding Quantity, which is what remains of it.
- Coverage Gap — For one Item of one Warehouse, how its total Outstanding Quantity divides into what the Warehouse already holds, what it has on order, and what is promised with neither behind it: On-hand Quantity, Inbound Quantity and Uncovered Quantity. It is the Demand Line's Coverage read as a quantity rather than as a list of the Purchase Drafts covering it. NOT `ordering`'s Coverage, which names each Purchase Draft Line linked to an Item's demand and states no arithmetic across them. NOT a shortage judgement, a reorder point or a safety-stock calculation, none of which this product holds.
- Inbound Quantity — For one Item of one Warehouse, the total ordered quantity of the Purchase Draft Lines naming that Item on Purchase Drafts still in Draft or Ready for Ordering, counting lines of both Delivery Modes because goods sent Direct to Customer answer the customer's demand exactly as goods reaching the dock do. NOT the stated quantity of the links between a Purchase Draft Line and the Customer Orders it was meant for, which `ordering` never reconciles with the line, with the Customer Order or with each other, and which therefore states an intention rather than a quantity on order. NOT the quantity that has arrived, which a Draft or Ready for Ordering line has not recorded.
- Uncovered Quantity — For one Item of one Warehouse, the part of its total Outstanding Quantity that neither On-hand Quantity nor Inbound Quantity accounts for, and nothing when the two together meet or exceed it. It is arithmetic over figures a member maintains and decisions a member made, so it reports what the records say rather than what is physically true. NOT a back order, which `ordering` uses for a Customer Order still waiting. NOT a stockout, a shortage or a fulfilment risk score, none of which this product defines.
- Demand Pressure — For one Warehouse, its total Outstanding Quantity divided into Urgency Bands. It says how much is promised and how soon, and says nothing about whether it can be supplied. NOT the Coverage Gap, which asks what stands behind the demand rather than when it is due.
- Urgency Band — Which of three bands one Customer Order's Outstanding Quantity falls into, read from its needed-by date against the day it is read: Overdue, its needed-by date having passed; Due soon, needed within the next fourteen days; or Later, needed after those fourteen days. The bands partition the demand so that each quantity appears in exactly one. The fourteen days are a stated span fixed by this feature's owners, no artifact in the product recording a lead time, a service window or any horizon one could be derived from. NOT a priority a member sets, and NOT a service level the product promises; the date the member recorded is the only input.
- Overdue — Of a Customer Order, that its needed-by date has passed while it is still Unfulfilled. Of a Purchase Draft, nothing at all — a draft whose Expected Arrival Date has passed is simply late and is named so. NOT cancelled and NOT failed; an Overdue Customer Order is owed exactly as it was before the date passed.
- Draft Age — How long one Purchase Draft has stood in the state it is in, counted from the moment it entered that state: from when it was created for a Draft, and from when it was made Ready for Ordering for a Ready for Ordering one. NOT the age of the Purchase Draft itself, which would make a draft readied yesterday after a month in Draft read as stale purchasing work when the purchasing work is a day old.
- Age Band — Which of four spans one Purchase Draft's Draft Age falls into: up to seven days, eight to fourteen, fifteen to thirty, or more than thirty. NOT a deadline, an escalation or an expiry; nothing happens to a draft because of the band it is in.
- Remainder Row — The single row a row-oriented Panel collapses everything below its top rows into, stating how many Items or Reasons it holds so that the largest bar on a chart is never anonymous. NOT an Other category a member assigns anything to, and NOT a rounding residue; it is the rest of the real rows, added up.
- On-time Arrival Rate — For one Warehouse, the share of its Via Warehouse Purchase Draft Lines on which something was received, whose ending was recorded no later than the Expected Arrival Date their Purchase Draft carried. A line on a draft carrying no Expected Arrival Date is undated, a line on which nothing was received recorded no arrival to be timely or late, and a Direct to Customer line never reaches the dock at all; each is counted in neither the numerator nor the denominator. NOT a supplier's performance, the product modelling no supplier; it is this Warehouse's record of goods reaching it when they were expected.
- Conformance Rate — For one Warehouse, the share of its Purchase Draft Lines carrying a recorded Pre-receipt Conformance whose verdict is Met, among those carrying Met or Not met. A line carrying Not applicable is excluded as having been given no instruction to honour, and a line carrying no verdict at all is unknown rather than failed and enters neither part of the figure. NOT a quality score for the goods, which Rejections record; this is whether the packaging instruction was followed.
- Receipt Reliability — For one Warehouse, its On-time Arrival Rate and its Conformance Rate read together against the quantity it received. Both rates and that quantity are read over the Warehouse's whole retained record rather than a recent period, no Panel on either Dashboard being period-bounded; the Conformance Rate therefore carries every ending recorded before `arrival-inspection` shipped, none of which was backfilled, which is what §7's conformance-coverage figure exists to expose. It is two independent shares set beside each other, never combined into one number. NOT a ranking, a grade or a threshold a Warehouse passes or fails.
- Reason Concentration — Across one Warehouse's Rejections, how much of the total Rejected Quantity the largest Rejection Reasons account for, read as each Reason's share and the running total down the order. It counts Rejections of both Sources — Inspected, judged at this Warehouse's own dock, and Customer-reported, refused by the end customer on a Direct to Customer line — and shows the Customer-reported part within each Reason, the two being recorded differently enough that `arrival-inspection` requires they never be confused. NOT a dock figure, which is why the Direct to Customer exclusion that governs the expected arrivals and the On-time Arrival Rate does not govern this Panel. NOT a cause; a Reason is what a member stated, neither derived nor verified, and a concentration says where to look rather than what is wrong.
- Assigned Quantity — For one Customer Order, how much of its Recorded Quantity every Allocation made to it has covered so far, whenever those Allocations were made. One Customer Order may have been allocated several times across several weeks, because an Allocation is made per link between a Purchase Draft Line and a Customer Order rather than once per order; Order Flow adds them and reports the total against the week the Customer Order was recorded. NOT a Customer Order becoming Fulfilled, which no record timestamps, and NOT a delivery to the customer, which the product does not model — an Allocation says the goods are that customer's, not that the customer has them.

## Invariants

- Every figure on either Dashboard is derived on read from records other features own. Nothing here is stored, nothing is cached beyond the reader's own session, and no figure is ever written back onto the records it was read from.
- Only Unfulfilled Customer Orders contribute to any figure of what is still owed. A cancelled Customer Order keeps the Outstanding Quantity it had when it was cancelled, so it is excluded by its state and never by its quantity. Order Flow is the one exception, and is not a figure of what is owed: it reports what each week took on, so a cancelled Customer Order stays in the week it was recorded and its cancelled quantity is shown as withdrawn from that week.
- Only Purchase Drafts in Draft or Ready for Ordering contribute to Inbound Quantity, to the Coverage Gap and to the Purchasing Pipeline — the Warehouse Panel — which is exactly the set `ordering`'s Coverage admits. To those figures a Closed or Discarded draft is history. Purchasing Spread, the Workspace Panel, is the single exception and counts drafts in every state, Discarded included, because its subject is where a Warehouse's purchasing work ends up rather than what it currently has on order.
- Inbound Quantity is read from the ordered quantities of Purchase Draft Lines, never from the stated quantities of the links between those lines and Customer Orders.
- Uncovered Quantity is never negative: an Item covered beyond its demand reports nothing uncovered rather than a surplus.
- No figure is produced by aggregating two independent one-to-many relationships in one grouping; each is aggregated in its own context and only then set beside the others, so no quantity is ever multiplied by the row count of another.
- A Direct to Customer Purchase Draft Line counts toward Inbound Quantity and toward no figure about the dock — not the expected arrivals of a week, not the On-time Arrival Rate, and not the received quantity a Warehouse is measured by. Reason Concentration is not a figure about the dock: it reports what was refused wherever it was refused, so a Customer-reported Rejection on a Direct to Customer line counts in it and is shown as its own Source.
- A Purchase Draft carrying no Expected Arrival Date is undated: it appears in no week and in no On-time Arrival Rate, and the count of undated drafts is stated rather than left to be inferred from a total that does not add up.
- Arrival Timing reads only the Purchase Drafts standing in Ready for Ordering at the moment it is read, because only a frozen draft's Expected Arrival Date is a commitment rather than a working note, and a draft that has since reached Closed or been Discarded is history whose date no longer describes goods awaited at the dock. A dated draft still in Draft therefore counts toward Inbound Quantity and appears in no week, and how many such drafts there are is stated on the Panel.
- A Purchase Draft Line on which nothing was received recorded no arrival, so it enters neither part of the On-time Arrival Rate — being neither timely nor late — exactly as it records neither a Condition Split nor a Pre-receipt Conformance.
- Every figure Order Flow presents belongs to the week the Customer Order was recorded. Nothing is placed in the week an Allocation or a cancellation happened, because a figure so placed could not be a part of the quantity recorded in any week.
- A Recorded Quantity is read as it stands, not as it stood. A member amending a Customer Order changes what a past week reports and no record keeps the earlier figure, so Order Flow states that its weeks move.
- A Purchase Draft Line carrying no Pre-receipt Conformance is unknown, never Not met, and a line recorded Not applicable was given no instruction to honour and enters no Conformance Rate.
- A Via Warehouse Purchase Draft Line whose ending has not been recorded enters neither part of the On-time Arrival Rate: nothing has yet said the goods reached the dock, and a line that never arrives is one its Purchase Draft was closed without.
- Every time axis is bucketed by week. No figure on either Dashboard is presented by day.
- Every figure on a Warehouse Dashboard belongs to exactly one Warehouse, and is authorized by the Role its reader holds in that Warehouse. The Workspace Dashboard sets Warehouses beside one another and pools no Item across them; no figure on it identifies an Item at all.
- No series, axis label, bucket or hover on either Dashboard is keyed by a Customer, a Customer name or a Delivery Address.
- A Panel whose underlying read the actor does not hold is absent rather than empty, and a Dashboard on which no Panel is permitted is a denial rather than a page of empty frames.
- Both Dashboards are read-only. Opening either one records nothing, changes no state and moves no quantity.

## Out of scope

- Configurable dashboards: adding, removing, resizing or reordering Panels, saved views, per-member layouts, and any choice of what a Dashboard shows. The four Panels of each Dashboard are fixed, which is what makes them the same four for everyone talking about them.
- Drill-through from a figure to the records behind it. A Panel names an Item, a Warehouse or a Reason and offers no route to the Demand Line or the Purchase Draft it was read from; the member goes to the destination that owns them.
- Export, download, scheduled delivery, emailed digests, and any form of the figures leaving the screen they are read on.
- Alerts, thresholds, targets, and any figure that judges a Warehouse rather than reporting it. Nothing on either Dashboard goes red, and no band is a service level.
- Monetary reporting of every kind: value, cost, margin, spend and currency. The product holds no price anywhere, so every figure here is a quantity.
- Stock-level history and any On-hand Quantity over time. On-hand Quantity is a maintained figure recorded as an absolute count, so no past balance can be reconstructed from what is stored.
- Comparing one Item across Warehouses, and any figure that treats the same SKU in two Warehouses as one thing. `ordering` makes a SKU name two unrelated Items in two Warehouses, and nothing here overrides that.
- Supplier scorecards and vendor ratings. The product models no supplier, so the Receipt Reliability of a Warehouse is that Warehouse's own record and attributes nothing to whoever sent the goods.
- Filtering, date-range selection and any control that changes what a Panel reads. A Dashboard is read as it is found.
- The Rejection Register and the Rejection Marker, which remain the committed follow-up to `arrival-inspection`. Reason Concentration is a glance at what is refused; the Register is the period-bounded, groupable read of it, and carrying a refusal's reason onto the demand it restored belongs to neither Dashboard.
