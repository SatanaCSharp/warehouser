# Domain Context — arrival-inspection

The roles and the domain objects this feature builds on are defined where they are owned and are not
repeated here: `Warehouse Member`, `Warehouse Manager`, `Role` and `Permission` in
[`access`](../access/CONTEXT.md); `Purchase Draft`, `Purchase Draft Line`, `Allocation`,
`Outstanding Quantity`, `Demand Line`, `Packaging Type`, `Pre-receipt Requirement`,
`Value-adding Note`, `Transit Zone`, `On-hand Quantity`, `Drift Signal`, `Fulfilled` and
`Unfulfilled` in [`ordering`](../ordering/CONTEXT.md); `Delivery Mode`, `Via Warehouse`,
`Direct to Customer`, `Direct Delivery`, `Customer` and `Address Drift` in
[`delivery-addresses`](../delivery-addresses/CONTEXT.md).

## Glossary

- Accepted Quantity — The part of a Purchase Draft Line's Received Quantity the Warehouse Member judged fit to go out to a customer, derived as the Received Quantity less every Rejection raised on that line rather than stated by the member. It is the only quantity an Allocation may draw on, which narrows the bound `ordering` sets at the Received Quantity. NOT a stated figure the member types, so it can never disagree with the Rejections beside it. NOT On-hand Quantity, which no part of this feature changes.
- Arrival Inspection — The judgement a Warehouse Member makes while recording a Purchase Draft Line's ending: the Condition Split together with the Pre-receipt Conformance. It is part of recording the ending and not a step that happens afterwards, so a line has its inspection exactly when it has its ending. NOT a quality procedure, a sampling plan, an approval by a second member, or a test of any kind, none of which this release provides. NOT a separate inspection status a line rests in, as it would be in a warehouse that quarantined goods and decided later.
- Condition Split — How a Purchase Draft Line's Received Quantity divides between its Accepted Quantity and its Rejections, stated by the member recording the ending and never afterwards changed. NOT the division of an arrived quantity across the Customer Orders waiting for it, which is the Allocation and divides by customer rather than by condition. NOT a stock status, which would need a movement ledger this release does not have.
- Customer-reported — The Rejection Source recorded when the goods were refused by the end customer rather than judged at our own dock, which is the only Source available on a Direct to Customer line because no member ever saw those goods. The member records what the customer told them and the system verifies none of it, on exactly the terms `delivery-addresses` already sets for a Direct Delivery. NOT a customer return, which would be a movement of goods this release does not model.
- Disposition — What the Warehouse decided became of the goods one Rejection refused: Refused at delivery, Held for return, Scrapped on site, or Undecided. It starts Undecided, may be changed afterwards by an authorized member without touching any quantity, and records the acting member and the time whenever it changes; once a decision has been recorded it may be corrected to another decision but is never returned to Undecided, and no decision is terminal. Every value is available on every Rejection whatever its Rejection Source, because a Disposition records what the Warehouse decided rather than where the goods physically were. NOT a Stock Movement, and NOT evidence the goods actually went anywhere — it records a decision, not an event. NOT a Delivery Mode, which says where goods travel rather than what became of them.
- Inspected — The Rejection Source recorded when a Warehouse Member judged the goods at the Warehouse's own dock, which is the only Source available on a Via Warehouse line. NOT a verification the system performed, which it never does; the member looked and stated what they saw.
- Pre-receipt Conformance — Whether the Packaging Type and Value-adding Note frozen on a Purchase Draft Line were honoured by the supplier, recorded once per line as Met, Not met, or Not applicable, with the member's note when Not met. A line frozen carrying a Packaging Type, a Value-adding Note, or both is recorded Met or Not met and never Not applicable, which belongs only to a line that was given neither instruction; one judgement covers both, so a line whose packaging was honoured and whose note was not is recorded Not met with the member's note naming which of the two failed. It is recorded with the line's ending and never afterwards changed, and it closes the non-goal `ordering` states when it excludes recording whether a Pre-receipt Requirement was met. NOT a Pre-receipt Requirement, which is the instruction given to the supplier rather than the judgement of whether they followed it. NOT a Drift Signal or Address Drift, both of which compare a frozen draft against current demand rather than against what physically arrived.
- Received Quantity — What the supplier presented at the dock for one Purchase Draft Line, counting goods the Warehouse went on to refuse and goods a sealed unit declared but did not hold. On a Direct to Customer line, where no member ever saw the goods, it is what the member records as delivered from the customer's own account, on exactly the terms `delivery-addresses` already sets for a Direct Delivery. Where refused goods went afterwards is the Disposition and never the arithmetic. NOT the quantity the Warehouse kept, which is the Accepted Quantity. NOT the quantity ordered, which it is bounded by neither above nor below.
- Rejected Quantity — One Purchase Draft Line's total across every Rejection raised on it. NOT a quantity that was never delivered, which is simply the difference between what was ordered and the Received Quantity and is refused by nobody.
- Rejection — A quantity of one Purchase Draft Line's arrival that the Warehouse refused, carrying exactly one Rejection Reason, one Rejection Source, and its own Rejection Record. One line's ending may hold several Rejections, each carrying a different Reason. A Rejection is never allocated and never reduces any Customer Order's Outstanding Quantity, so the customer it was meant for keeps waiting. NOT the system refusing an operation, which is what "refused" means everywhere else in these specifications; a Rejection is a member's judgement about goods, recorded successfully. NOT a Return, a supplier claim, a debit note, or a chargeback, none of which this release models.
- Rejection Reason — Which entry of the system-managed catalogue one Rejection carries, stated by the member and neither derived nor verified by the system: Damaged in transit, Damaged by packing, Quality defect, Wrong item supplied, Short within packaging, Packaging not as instructed, Value-adding note not applied, Shelf life insufficient, Documentation missing, or Unfit — other, which always carries the member's description. The catalogue is extended only by the team, exactly as the Packaging Type catalogue is, and never by a member — and extension is the only change it admits, no entry ever being reworded or retired, so a Rejection names its Reason rather than copying its wording and a closed draft always reads as the catalogue reads. NOT a snapshot taken at the moment of recording, unlike the Packaging Type frozen on a line, which it does not need to be precisely because no wording ever changes beneath it. NOT the reason stated when a Purchase Draft is closed, when a Customer Order is cancelled, or when On-hand Quantity is adjusted, each of which is free text about a different act.
- Rejection Record — What one Rejection carries beyond its quantity and its Rejection Reason: the member's description, its Rejection Source, its Disposition, and the member who raised it with the time they did. Its quantity, Reason, Source and line are fixed once recorded; its description and its Disposition may be amended afterwards by an authorized member. NOT an audit trail of the Rejection, which this release does not keep beyond the acting member and time each amendment records.
- Rejection Source — Whether one Rejection was Inspected at our own dock or Customer-reported, fixed when the Rejection is recorded. It follows from the line's Delivery Mode — a Via Warehouse line admits only Inspected and a Direct to Customer line only Customer-reported — and is stated so the two are never confused when the Rejections on a closed draft are read together. NOT a Delivery Mode, which is a property of the line saying where the goods travelled; the Source is a property of the Rejection saying who found the problem, and it is recorded even though it can be inferred so that a Rejection read on its own says which it is.

## Invariants

- For every Purchase Draft Line whose ending has been recorded, the Received Quantity is what the supplier presented, the Accepted Quantity is that figure less every Rejection on the line, and every one of those quantities is a whole number that is never negative. Rejections totalling more than the Received Quantity are refused, and a line where nothing arrived records nothing received and neither a Condition Split nor a Pre-receipt Conformance.
- An Allocation never exceeds the Accepted Quantity of the Purchase Draft Line it is made from. Every other bound `ordering` sets on an Allocation is unchanged: never more than a Customer Order's Outstanding Quantity, and never to one that has been cancelled or is already Fulfilled.
- Every Rejection carries exactly one Rejection Reason and a quantity greater than nothing. Two Rejections on one Purchase Draft Line never carry the same Reason.
- A Rejection carrying the Unfit — other Reason always carries the member's description.
- A Purchase Draft Line whose Pre-receipt Conformance is recorded as Met carries no Rejection for Packaging not as instructed or Value-adding note not applied, and a line carrying either of those Rejections is never recorded as Met.
- A Purchase Draft Line frozen carrying a Packaging Type, a Value-adding Note, or both records its Pre-receipt Conformance as Met or Not met; only a line frozen carrying neither records Not applicable.
- A Rejection's description and the note carried by a Not met Pre-receipt Conformance each stay within one thousand characters.
- Every Rejection on a Via Warehouse line carries the Inspected Source, and every Rejection on a Direct to Customer line carries the Customer-reported Source.
- The Condition Split and the Pre-receipt Conformance are recorded exactly once, at the moment the Purchase Draft Line's ending is recorded, and neither is ever changed afterwards. No Rejection is raised against a line whose ending has already been recorded.
- A Rejection's quantity, Rejection Reason, Rejection Source and Purchase Draft Line are fixed once recorded. Its description and its Disposition may be amended afterwards by an authorized member, and each amendment records the acting member and the time.
- A Disposition once decided is never returned to Undecided, though it may be corrected to another decision.
- The Rejection Reason catalogue is extended only: no entry is ever reworded or retired, so the Reason a recorded Rejection carries never changes beneath it.
- The Condition Split, the Pre-receipt Conformance, every Rejection, every Allocation, and the line's ending are recorded together or not at all.
- Every Rejection belongs to exactly one Warehouse — that of the Purchase Draft Line it names — and every operation on one is authorized by the Role its actor holds in that Warehouse. Nothing this feature reads crosses that boundary, including across the Warehouses of one Workspace.
- Nothing in this feature changes On-hand Quantity, which moves only when a member sets it to a counted figure while stating a reason.

## Out of scope

- Suppliers as records, supplier scorecards, and vendor performance ratings; the product models no supplier at all, so a Rejection names what was refused and why and never who sent it.
- Claims, debit notes, chargebacks, return authorization numbers, and any financial recovery from a supplier; the product holds no money and no counterparty.
- Returning refused goods as a modelled movement, quarantine areas, and put-away; there is no Stock Movement ledger and no Locations, and a Disposition records what a member decided rather than that goods went anywhere.
- Accepting refused goods by agreement with the customer at a reduction, which would need the customer's agreement and a pricing model this product does not have.
- Raising a Rejection after a Purchase Draft Line's ending has been recorded, including damage found when a carton is opened days later; an ending is recorded once, and a post-arrival change of condition belongs with the Stock Movement ledger a later release introduces.
- Automatically re-ordering a refused shortfall; a member decides what to buy, and the shortfall reappearing on the Demand Line is the signal that decision needs.
- Photographic Evidence attached to a Rejection; the product has no binary-asset surface of any kind, and a photograph has no redacted form, so a member permitted to read a Rejection could read from an image the customer identity every other read redacts field by field. It is deferred to its own feature with its own storage, retention and disclosure decisions.
- The Rejection Register, and the Rejection Marker that would carry a refusal's reason onto the demand it restored; both are read surfaces over records this release already creates, and both are deferred to an immediate follow-up. Until then a refusal's reason reaches only a member who opens the closed Purchase Draft it lives on.
