# Domain Context — ordering

## Glossary

- Allocation — The record of how much of an arrived quantity a Warehouse Member assigned to each Customer Order linked to a Purchase Draft Line, decided by that member rather than derived by the system. Recording it reduces the Outstanding Quantity of each Customer Order it names. NOT a reservation, which would claim demand before goods exist; an Allocation is only ever recorded against goods that have already arrived. NOT a shipment, which this release does not provide — an Allocation says the goods in the Transit Zone are that customer's, not that the customer has them.
- Arrival Confirmation — The act by which a Warehouse Member records that the goods of a Purchase Draft physically reached the Transit Zone, stating the received quantity of each Purchase Draft Line and the Allocation of that quantity across the Customer Orders the line is linked to. It is the one operation permitted to write to a frozen Purchase Draft, and it writes only the received quantities, the Allocations, and the move to Closed. It happens once for a draft and closes it: a received quantity may fall short of or exceed what was ordered, and goods the supplier still owes are ordered on a new draft rather than awaited on this one. A line whose linked Customer Orders have all been cancelled or Fulfilled is confirmed with its received quantity and no Allocation at all. NOT a receiving process with put-away, inspection, or Locations, none of which this release provides. NOT the moment On-hand Quantity changes, which happens only through an adjustment stating its reason.
- Back Order — A synonym for an Unfulfilled Customer Order. Used when the emphasis is on the waiting rather than on the record. NOT a separate entity.
- Coverage — What a Demand Line's linked Purchase Draft Lines say about it: how much of it some draft claims to serve and which drafts those are, read from the quantity the member stated on each link. Coverage is informational and never blocks a member from drafting the same demand again. A Purchase Draft that has reached Closed or been Discarded presents as Coverage no longer, whichever way it got there. NOT Allocation, which is decided only at Arrival Confirmation against goods that exist and which is the only thing that reduces demand.
- Customer Order — A record that a named end customer is waiting for a stated quantity of one Item in one Warehouse by a stated date, carrying the Outstanding Quantity still owed to that customer. It is the unit of demand this feature consolidates. NOT a sales order with pricing, payment, or fulfilment, none of which this release provides. NOT a Purchase Draft, which is what the Warehouse orders from its supplier.
- Demand Line — One Item's consolidated Unfulfilled demand in one Warehouse: the total Outstanding Quantity across its Unfulfilled Customer Orders together with the earliest date any of them is needed by. It is a derived view of Customer Orders, never a stored record a member edits. NOT a Purchase Draft Line, which is what the member decided to order in response.
- Demand Snapshot — The quantity, needed-by date, and state of every Customer Order a Purchase Draft's lines link to, captured at the moment the draft entered Ready for Ordering. It is what a Drift Signal compares current demand against, so drift reports what actually differs now rather than merely that something was once touched. NOT part of the frozen order itself, which is what the member told the supplier; the snapshot is what the demand looked like when they told them.
- Discarded — The state a Purchase Draft reaches when a member abandons it before it was ever made ready. A Discarded draft stops appearing among the drafts being worked on and presents as Coverage no longer, while the Customer Orders its lines linked to are left exactly as they were. NOT Closed, which a draft reaches only after it was frozen, and which records either what arrived or why the supplier could not fulfil it.
- Drift Signal — The indication shown against a Ready for Ordering Purchase Draft that the demand its lines are linked to has changed since the draft was frozen — a linked Customer Order cancelled, reduced, increased, re-dated, or become Fulfilled through the arrival of a different Purchase Draft. It is a comparison against the Demand Snapshot the freeze captured, so a value amended and then put back as it was reports no drift. It reports the divergence and never rewrites the frozen draft. NOT an error, and NOT an instruction; what to do about it is the member's decision.
- Expected Arrival Date — The date a Warehouse Member records on a Purchase Draft as when they expect its goods to reach the Transit Zone. It is the member's own estimate, stated before they contact the supplier, and this release neither derives nor verifies it. NOT a commitment made to an end customer, and NOT a date the supplier has confirmed. It is frozen with the rest of the draft at Ready for Ordering.
- Fulfilled — The state a Customer Order reaches when its Outstanding Quantity falls to nothing through Allocation. A Fulfilled Customer Order leaves the consolidated demand and is no longer counted in any Demand Line, until a member raises its quantity again, which makes it Unfulfilled once more and returns it to the consolidated demand. NOT Cancelled, which ends a Customer Order because the customer no longer wants the goods rather than because they were covered.
- Inactive — The state of an Item a member has deactivated because it should no longer be used. An Inactive Item is not offered when demand is recorded or a draft is assembled, while every Customer Order and Purchase Draft Line already naming it stays readable and keeps counting exactly as before, and its SKU stays taken. NOT deleted, which this release does not provide. NOT a merge of two Items naming one physical good, which is out of scope.
- Item — A distinct good the Warehouse can be asked for, identified by a SKU that is unique within that Warehouse and carrying a description and the Unit of Measure its quantities are counted in. It is the thing Customer Orders and Purchase Draft Lines both name, and therefore the axis demand consolidates on. It is active when created and may be made Inactive and active again. NOT a stocked product with Locations and movement history, which a later release provides. NOT a catalogue shared across the Workspace; each Warehouse maintains its own.
- On-hand Quantity — The quantity of one Item a Warehouse Member states is physically present in that Warehouse's Transit Zone, changed only by that member setting it to a count they have made and recording a reason. It is stated as the count itself, never as an increase or decrease applied to a previous figure, and a newly created Item starts with nothing on hand. It is a maintained figure, not a computed one, and this release deliberately provides no ledger that could contradict or reconstruct it. NOT a Stock balance, which a later release derives from Stock Movements.
- Outstanding Quantity — How much of a Customer Order the customer is still waiting for: the quantity recorded, less every Allocation made to it. A member amending the Customer Order may not reduce its quantity below what has already been assigned to it, so this figure never has to be floored. NOT the quantity originally recorded, which never changes except when the member amends the Customer Order.
- Packaging Type — How goods of one Purchase Draft Line must physically arrive, chosen from the system-defined catalogue: Loose Items, Cartons, Pallets, or Cable Coil. NOT a Unit of Measure, which describes how the quantity is counted rather than how the goods are presented. NOT free text; the catalogue is system-managed like the Permission catalogue.
- Pre-receipt Requirement — The instruction a Purchase Draft Line carries about how its goods must arrive so they can be cross-docked straight to the customer: a Packaging Type together with a Value-adding Note. It is stated for the supplier's benefit and is never verified by this release. NOT a Packaging Type alone, which is only one part of it.
- Purchase Draft — The list of goods a Warehouse Member decided to order from a supplier, belonging to exactly one Warehouse, moving from Draft to Ready for Ordering to Closed, or from Draft to Discarded. While it is in the Draft state its lines, links, and Pre-receipt Requirements are assembled and revised over the course of deciding rather than fixed in one submission. From Ready for Ordering onward it is a frozen record of what the member told the supplier, not a live projection of current demand. NOT a purchase order, which would be priced and transmitted; this release transmits nothing and the member contacts the supplier themselves.
- Purchase Draft Line — One Item and quantity within a Purchase Draft, carrying its own Pre-receipt Requirement and its own links to the Customer Orders it is meant to serve, each link stating how much of the line the member intends for that customer. NOT a Demand Line, which is derived from Customer Orders rather than decided by a member.
- Ready for Ordering — The state a Purchase Draft enters when its member has finished deciding and is about to contact the supplier. Entering it freezes the draft's lines, quantities, links, Pre-receipt Requirements, and Expected Arrival Date, captures the Demand Snapshot, and starts Drift Signal reporting against it. NOT an approval, since no second member is involved. NOT a sent order, since nothing leaves the system.
- SKU — The identifier a Warehouse uses for one Item, unique within that Warehouse and chosen by its members. It may be corrected only while no Customer Order and no Purchase Draft Line names the Item; once one does, it is fixed for the life of the Item, and deactivating that Item does not release it for reuse. NOT a globally unique product code such as a manufacturer barcode, which this release does not model.
- Transit Zone — The physical part of the Warehouse where goods rest between arriving and going out to the customer. Because Warehouser treats the Warehouse as a cross-docking point, this is the only place goods are expected to be, and what it holds is usually near zero. NOT a Location, which a later release introduces as a modelled place; the Transit Zone is the whole Warehouse in this release and is not a record.
- Unfulfilled — The state of a Customer Order that has Outstanding Quantity left and has not been cancelled. It is the single word this feature uses for demand that still counts; "open" and "outstanding order" are not used as synonyms for it. NOT Fulfilled, and NOT Cancelled.
- Unit of Measure — How an Item's quantities are counted — pieces, metres, kilograms, litres — stated on the Item so that every quantity naming it is read the same way by everyone. NOT a Packaging Type, which says how goods are physically presented on arrival rather than how they are counted; an Item counted in metres may arrive as a Cable Coil.
- Value-adding Note — Free text on a Purchase Draft Line describing processing the goods need before or on arrival, such as a translated sticker, a customer-specific barcode, bundling, or an inserted manual. NOT a place for prices, supplier contacts, or payment terms, which this release excludes from the product.

## Invariants

- Every Item, Customer Order, and Purchase Draft belongs to exactly one Warehouse, and every operation on one is authorized by the Role its actor holds in that Warehouse. An archived Warehouse authorizes no operation that changes what it holds, while its watch capabilities keep authorizing reads on exactly the Permission terms that applied before it was archived.
- A SKU identifies at most one Item within a Warehouse, and the same SKU in two Warehouses names two unrelated Items. A SKU is correctable only while no Customer Order and no Purchase Draft Line names its Item, and deactivating an Item never releases its SKU for reuse.
- Every Item carries exactly one Unit of Measure, and every Customer Order and every Purchase Draft Line names exactly one existing Item of its own Warehouse. A Customer Order and a Purchase Draft Line are only ever created naming an Item that is active; an Item that goes Inactive leaves the records already naming it untouched.
- A Customer Order and a Purchase Draft Line may only be linked when both belong to the same Warehouse.
- Linking is many-to-many and claims nothing: a Purchase Draft Line may link to no Customer Orders or to several, a Customer Order may be linked by several Purchase Draft Lines, and each link carries a quantity the member states that is never required to reconcile with the Purchase Draft Line quantity, with the Customer Order quantity, or with any other link.
- A Customer Order is recorded with a needed-by date that is not already past; amending it moves it to another date that is not already past.
- A Purchase Draft reaches Ready for Ordering only when it holds at least one Purchase Draft Line.
- Entering Ready for Ordering captures the Demand Snapshot, and a Purchase Draft in Ready for Ordering or Closed never has its lines, ordered quantities, links, Pre-receipt Requirements, Expected Arrival Date, or Demand Snapshot changed; divergence from current demand is reported as a Drift Signal instead. The only writes a frozen draft accepts are those of Arrival Confirmation — the received quantity of each line and its Allocations — and its move to Closed with the closure reason.
- A Purchase Draft reaches Closed only through Arrival Confirmation or through a member closing it with a reason, and Arrival Confirmation happens at most once for a draft. A draft still in the Draft state is never Closed; a member abandons it by Discarding it instead, and a draft that has been made ready is never Discarded.
- Every ordered and demanded quantity is a positive whole number. Every received quantity, Allocation, Outstanding Quantity, and On-hand Quantity is a whole number that is never negative, so a line where nothing arrived records nothing received.
- The quantity received for a Purchase Draft Line is whatever physically arrived and is bounded neither above nor below by the quantity ordered. The Allocation recorded for that line never exceeds the quantity received for it, the Allocation made to one Customer Order never exceeds its Outstanding Quantity, and an Allocation is only ever made to a linked Customer Order that is still Unfulfilled — so received goods whose linked demand has all gone are recorded with no Allocation at all.
- Allocation is the only operation that reduces a Customer Order's Outstanding Quantity; a Customer Order whose Outstanding Quantity falls to nothing becomes Fulfilled and leaves the consolidated demand, and a member raising the quantity of a Fulfilled Customer Order makes it Unfulfilled again. A Customer Order's quantity is never amended below what has already been assigned to it.
- On-hand Quantity changes only through a member setting it to a counted figure while recording the acting member, the time, and a reason; it is never expressed as an increase or decrease, a newly created Item starts with nothing on hand, and nothing else in this release changes it, Arrival Confirmation included.
- Demand Lines and Coverage are derived from Customer Orders and Purchase Draft Lines on every read, never stored and never edited directly.
- The Packaging Type catalogue is system-managed and is extended only through application migrations.

## Out of scope

- Suppliers as records, supplier pricing, purchase-order transmission, and any electronic exchange with a supplier; the member contacts the supplier outside the product.
- Stock Movements, Stock balances, Locations, and put-away; this release provides only the manager-maintained On-hand Quantity that the buffer check needs. How that figure is reconciled when the Stock Movement ledger lands is open — see `spec.md` §8.
- Shipping or dispatching goods to the end customer, and any proof that a Pre-receipt Requirement was actually met on arrival.
- Automatic reorder points, demand forecasting, and any system-proposed order quantity; every quantity in a Purchase Draft is decided by a member.
- Consolidating demand across several Warehouses of one Workspace, and any customer record shared between Warehouses.
- Merging two Items that turn out to name the same physical good is not provided by this release; whether it becomes a later capability is open — see `spec.md` §8. Deactivating a single Item that should not be used _is_ provided; what is excluded is combining two of them into one and reusing a freed SKU.
